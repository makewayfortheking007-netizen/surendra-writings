WORTHY HUMANS

A work in progress
Work in progress

“I am going to kill that bastard! Who does he think he is?” Raghav yells as he enters the room, while Veer closes the door behind him.

“How the hell did this dimwit rise to a managerial position?” laments Raghav as Veer hands him a cold water bottle from the fridge.

Raghav has been working as a customer support representative for a tech company that he joined about three months ago. He goes at 9 and comes back by 8, all worked up and tired. His boss keeps demanding more out of him, always criticising his methods. The tucked-in shirt is out, and the shoes are dirty. Resembling his mental and physical state.

“For the amount of money they give you, it's okay for them to exploit you a little bit”, says Veer as he chuckles while he sits on the mat working on his laptop.

“Yeah, easy for you to say, you lazy, unemployed piece of shit.”

“ Ayy, don't call me lazy or unemployed; I am an aspirant. Put some respect on that,” says Veer while adjusting his tie imaginatively.

Veer has been preparing for banking exams for the past year and has been able to clear the pre once. Straight after graduation, he set his eyes on the government exam, chasing a stable job that gives him a pension when he is no longer able to work. While he denies it's his father's wish for him to take up a government job because he thinks in India, a government job brings stability that no other job can. Veer just wants to pass the exam, but deep down, he just wants to be seen. To become the person his parents always wanted him to be.

Raghav loosens his tie, takes his shoes off, and leaves them at the door itself.

“It's 9 pm already, where's that aspiring desi Wes Anderson? He was supposed to bring some food, wasn't he?”

Veer doesn't respond immediately. His eyes are glued to the screen, and he is frantically scrolling.

“Got your hands on some secret documents or what? What are you scrolling?”

Veer stops and pauses for a minute; he doesn’t say anything. There is silence in the room, only the sound of Raghav shuffling around the room.

“ I missed the cutoff by one mark”, says Veer with a poker face and hangs his head.

It was Veer’s second attempt at the banking job, having failed to clear the first attempt.

Raghav glanced over while he washed his face in a small basin that was the only new and pristine piece of accessory they had in their small 1bhk room in a tightly packed student area in Lane 4 of Laxmi Nagar.

“Shit happens” says Raghav with no expression on his face.

There is silence again, and only the sound of running water can be heard. As soon as silence starts taking over the room, the room is filled with a sharp shrill of the doorbell.

Raghav flinches as the sound makes his ears ring. He opens the door hurriedly.

“Can't you just ring the bell once and wait patiently?” says Raghav.

At the door is Akhil, holding up two polythene bags. Raghav can smell the steaming hot momos as the fresh smell of chutney hits his nostrils, and he grabs the bags from Akhil.

“You're welcome”, says Akhil as he smirks.

“ 3 plates of freshly made MOMS, Akhil laughs at his own joke.

It's from their favourite stall that has got the spelling of momos all wrong, but they haven't messed up the recipes and serve lip-smacking momos with spicy chutney that makes you sweat and leaves you panting for water as you gulp down more of them.

Akhil is from Delhi and lives with his family. He always had a passion for filmmaking, but did engineering as he wasn't really aware that filmmaking was his true calling. In his four years of graduation, he understood one thing, and that was that he shouldn't have done engineering. Akhil didn't sit for placement, and with the kind of marks he had, he knew his chances were slim. He started doing freelancing work for a firm.
