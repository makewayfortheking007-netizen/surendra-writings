UNTITLED

A work in progress
Work in progress

The music was so loud that even after Arjun had come out onto the balcony, he could hear the loud thumping music. As the breeze picked up, the volume subsided. He was sitting there alone with a bottle of water, sipping silently. Arjun didn't enjoy these parties at all: loud music, people drinking more than they could handle. He didn't judge them, but he didn't like it either.

His boss hosted the party, and Arjun had been expected to attend. So here he was, sitting alone on the penthouse balcony enjoying a brief respite from all the noise.

The sound of a sliding door broke the silence. He turned back and saw a girl walk out with headphones around her neck and a can of beer in her hand. Arjun wanted to ignore her, but he couldn't. The dim light spilling across her face revealed her striking features: beady eyes, a perfectly shaped nose that looked as though Michelangelo himself had sculpted it, and a toned physique that hinted at countless hours spent in the gym. The tiny nose ring suited her perfectly, making her more captivating.

He found himself staring at her and immediately looked the other way.

She closed the door behind her and stood a few feet away from him. Neither of them said a word. The silence lingered for a few seconds before the hiss of the can opening gently broke it.

She stood in silence, enjoying the pleasant breeze, when she finally spoke.

"Only a psychopath would choose plain water at a party like this," she remarked nonchalantly.

Arjun chuckled. He looked at the bottle, then at her, and replied,

"Someone has to remember what happens today."

She took a sip from the can and said, in a sarcastic tone,

"Oh, you must be so much fun at parties."

Arjun smirked, raised the bottle slightly, and said,

"I've heard better compliments."

She walked up to him and extended her hand.

"Hi, I'm Diya. Your DJ for this boring night."

“Hi, I am Arjun.”

They shook hands briefly.

She set the beer can aside and reached behind her back and unbuckled her hair. It fell over her shoulders, the silky strands started dancing with the breeze like waves rolling gently onto the shore again and again.

For a moment, everything around Arjun seemed to fade, things slowed down, and it seemed as if he was in a trance. He was aware that his gaze was fixated on her but couldn't stop himself. It caught him off guard. This didn't happen often. In fact, he couldn't remember the last time a woman had held his attention without much effort.

Realizing he was staring, he blinked, cleared his throat, and lowered his gaze to the floor.

“So Arjun, why are you up here all alone, sipping water?” She asked while tying her hair in a bun and putting that one stubborn hair strand behind her ear.

“Uh... no particular reason,” Arjun replied sheepishly. “I just couldn't handle it in there the chatter, the loud music. I needed a breather, so I came up here.”

“I see,” Diya said, gazing at the night sky.

Arjun studied her for a minute.

“You look tired”. I thought DJs had unlimited energy. He paused before adding, “By the way, your set was really smooth.”

“That's generous of you. And thank you.” She tilted her head. “Though if my set was that smooth, why are you out here enjoying the silence?”

“No, I didn't mean it like that,” Arjun shook his head “I just–”

“Relax, Arjun, I am kidding,” She said with a faint smile.

“To be honest,” she continued, “I'm up here for pretty much the same reason. Even I need to disappear for a few minutes sometimes. It helps me quiet my mind and gather my thoughts.”

“That actually makes sense,” Arjun said, easing into the chair.

“Well, I'd better get back. See you inside.”

Arjun raised his water bottle in salute.

“Cheers.”

She smiled and walked inside.

Arjun sat there in silence, the cool night breeze brushing against his face. Somehow, the evening had taken a turn in ways he couldn't explain. Their conversation kept replaying in his head the sound of her voice, the way she carried herself, the breeze catching her hair, the faint smile she wore. It had lasted only a few minutes, yet it felt like the beginning of something he couldn't quite fathom. Whatever it was, he knew one thing he wasn't ready for it to end.
